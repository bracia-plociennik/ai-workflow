## Findings

- **P0 — Refund reentrancy can drain other depositors’ funds.** [`refund()` sends Ether before clearing the caller’s balance](/private/tmp/pse-cli-baseline-blockchain-review-h22e2n3g/checkout/ai-workflow-workspace/projects/prompt-and-skill-efficiency-v1/evals/fixtures/blockchain-review/case.md:20). A depositor contract can call `refund()` again from its receive function while its balance is still recorded. For example, with a 1 ETH attacker deposit and 2 ETH from others, three bounded refund calls can pay the attacker 3 ETH and leave the other 2 ETH liability unpaid. Clear the balance before the external call; a failed send will revert that change.

- **P1 — Anyone can open refunds.** [`openRefunds()` has no authorization check](/private/tmp/pse-cli-baseline-blockchain-review-h22e2n3g/checkout/ai-workflow-workspace/projects/prompt-and-skill-efficiency-v1/evals/fixtures/blockchain-review/case.md:14). Any address can trigger the refund state. The authorized role and intended timing need an escrow specification before a fix is implemented.

The normal refund path clears a balance after payment, and a failed payment reverts. The accounting failure occurs when the recipient reenters before that clearing step. Deposits also remain possible after refunds open; whether that is allowed is unspecified.
