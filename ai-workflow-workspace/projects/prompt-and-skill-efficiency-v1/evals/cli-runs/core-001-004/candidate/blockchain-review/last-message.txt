## Findings

1. **P1 — Refunds can pay the same balance more than once.** In [case.md](/private/tmp/pse-cli-candidate-blockchain-review-w70l8wz_/checkout/ai-workflow-workspace/projects/prompt-and-skill-efficiency-v1/evals/fixtures/blockchain-review/case.md:20), `refund()` sends ETH before clearing `balances[msg.sender]`. A contract recipient can call `refund()` again from its fallback while its recorded balance is unchanged. For example, with 1 ETH credited to the attacker and 2 ETH credited to another depositor, two refund calls can pay the attacker 2 ETH and leave only 1 ETH backing the other depositor’s 2 ETH claim. Clear the balance before the external call; a failed send will revert that state change.

2. **P1 — Anyone can open refunds.** [openRefunds()](/private/tmp/pse-cli-candidate-blockchain-review-w70l8wz_/checkout/ai-workflow-workspace/projects/prompt-and-skill-efficiency-v1/evals/fixtures/blockchain-review/case.md:14) has no authorization check. Any address can enable withdrawals, regardless of the intended escrow decision. The authorized caller and conditions for opening refunds need an explicit policy enforced by the contract.

**Review limits:** This was a static, read-only review of the fixture. There is no accepted escrow specification or test harness here, so the intended refund trigger is unknown. I ran no Solidity tests, deployment, or provider calls. If a recipient rejects the ETH transfer, `require(ok)` reverts the refund and leaves its balance recorded; that failure path does not address the reentrant path.

**Review completeness:** Adversarial paths checked: unauthorized opening, repeated refund during a callback, empty balance, closed refunds, and failed transfer. The two findings block a quality-ready verdict. This advisory review does not issue formal `PASS` or `FAIL`.
