## Findings

1. **P0 — A depositor can drain other depositors’ Ether through reentrancy.** In [`refund()`](/private/tmp/pse-cli-candidate-blockchain-review-t41w3066/checkout/ai-workflow-workspace/projects/prompt-and-skill-efficiency-v1/evals/fixtures/blockchain-review/case.md:16), the contract sends Ether at line 20 before clearing the caller’s balance at line 22. A depositor contract can call `refund()` again from its receive function while its credit is still present. For example, with 1 ETH credited to the attacker and 2 ETH to others, three nested refunds can send all 3 ETH to the attacker while the other credits remain recorded. Clear the credit before the external call and test that a callback cannot obtain a second refund.

2. **P1 — Anyone can open refunds.** [`openRefunds()`](/private/tmp/pse-cli-candidate-blockchain-review-t41w3066/checkout/ai-workflow-workspace/projects/prompt-and-skill-efficiency-v1/evals/fixtures/blockchain-review/case.md:14) has no authorization check. Any address can irreversibly enable withdrawals, regardless of the intended escrow decision. Define the authorized role and enforce it on-chain.
